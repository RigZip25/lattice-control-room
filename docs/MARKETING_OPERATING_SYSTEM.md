# Marketing operating system

## Ambition

LATTICE is intended to operate as an elite full-cycle marketing organization,
not a junior campaign assistant. Its quality bar is the best available
professional practice, continuously tested against product-specific outcomes.
It must be capable of serving Lafwiron and, later, external product workspaces.

"Elite" is an operating standard, not a self-awarded score. LATTICE must show
the evidence, provenance, uncertainty and measured outcomes behind its advice.

## Responsibilities

### 1. Product and market intelligence

- understand the product, business model, margins and constraints;
- research customers, jobs-to-be-done, alternatives and competition;
- segment markets without turning demographic proxies into behavior;
- form positioning, category and entry hypotheses;
- maintain a versioned knowledge base of universal, category and local priors.

### 2. Growth strategy

- select acquisition, activation, monetization, retention and referral goals;
- define product-specific funnels and value events;
- choose channel portfolios according to intent, audience, economics and stage;
- protect exploration while reallocating capital toward evidence-supported work;
- diagnose product or retention problems instead of mislabeling all weakness as
  an acquisition problem.

### 3. Creative intelligence and production

- develop concepts, narratives, offers, hooks and proof structures;
- generate or coordinate photo, video, audio, design, landing pages, articles,
  email, social and other formats through replaceable capability providers;
- localize meaning and cultural context, not merely translate words;
- preserve asset lineage from hypothesis through brief, source inputs,
  production recipe, QA, distribution and outcome;
- detect fatigue and turn winners into falsifiable next variants.

### 4. Distribution and channel operations

- support paid, owned, earned, partner, community, search, lifecycle, referral
  and future channels through a capability registry;
- model each channel's native economics and metrics instead of forcing a common
  advertising vocabulary;
- manage accounts, permissions, policy constraints, frequency, sequencing and
  cross-channel coordination;
- keep external execution behind scoped authorization and auditable adapters.

### 5. SEO and editorial systems

- research search demand and topic/entity structure;
- design technical, programmatic and editorial SEO where appropriate;
- create useful content with source and claim provenance;
- connect rankings and traffic to downstream product value rather than treating
  impressions as the objective;
- maintain, consolidate and retire content as evidence changes.

### 6. Lifecycle, CRM and retention

- define activation and first-value milestones;
- orchestrate onboarding, education, reminders, win-back and referral flows;
- segment by observed product behavior and consented data;
- diagnose liquidity, trust, value, workflow, quality and frequency failures;
- optimize for durable customer value, not messaging volume.

### 7. Measurement and learning

- distinguish observation, inference and forecast;
- use incremental/marginal economics rather than vanity averages;
- pre-register success, stop and invalidation conditions;
- preserve negative results and contradictory evidence;
- measure forecast calibration and update versioned priors/playbooks without
  rewriting history;
- escalate causal claims only when the experimental design supports them.

## Capability model

LATTICE owns the decision logic and quality contract. A capability provider may
be an internal module, human specialist, AI model, SaaS API, advertising network
or publishing platform. Providers declare:

- supported operations and formats;
- input/output schemas;
- cost and latency estimates;
- data/privacy region and retention behavior;
- idempotency and reconciliation support;
- account and authorization requirements;
- quality/conformance evidence;
- failure and degradation semantics.

No provider is globally preferred. Selection is scoped by task, workspace,
brand, market, policy, quality, cost, latency and observed reliability.

## Quality system

Every material deliverable passes the relevant gates: factual/claim review,
brand fit, audience fit, originality, accessibility, platform policy, legal and
privacy constraints, localization, technical validity, experiment integrity and
measurement readiness. Production capacity never creates an obligation to
publish weak work.
